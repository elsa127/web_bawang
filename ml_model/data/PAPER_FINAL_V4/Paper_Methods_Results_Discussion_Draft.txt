METHODS DRAFT

Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance imagery from the
COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED collection was used for July-August
2025. Eight predictor variables were retained: B2, B3, B4, B8,
B11, B12, NDVI, and NDWI.

An XGBoost binary classifier was developed to discriminate
shallot and non-shallot agricultural land. Because multiple
pixels could originate from the same reference location,
model validation was conducted at the independent-site level
rather than by randomly splitting individual pixels.

A four-fold stratified site-level cross-validation procedure
was used. All pixels belonging to the same Parent_Site were
assigned to the same fold to prevent spatial information
leakage. During model fitting, pixel weights accounted for
the number of pixels contributed by each site, ground-truth
confidence, and class imbalance at the site level.

Out-of-fold probabilities were aggregated to independent
sites using the median probability of pixels within each site.
Classification performance was evaluated at a probability
threshold of 0.50. The reported metrics include
accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, specificity,
F1-score, Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC), ROC-AUC,
and precision-recall AUC. Stratified bootstrap resampling
(2000 iterations) was used to estimate
95% confidence intervals while preserving the observed
number of positive and negative reference sites.


RESULTS DRAFT

The quality-controlled dataset used for model evaluation
contained 40 independent reference sites, including
32 shallot sites and 8
non-shallot sites. These sites contributed 72 pixel
samples to model development.

At the fixed 0.50 probability threshold, site-level
out-of-fold evaluation produced an accuracy of
0.475, balanced accuracy of
0.344, precision of
0.720, recall of
0.562, specificity
of 0.125, and F1-score of
0.632. The Matthews correlation
coefficient was -0.258.

Probability-based discrimination was limited, with a ROC-AUC
of 0.305. The precision-recall
average precision was 0.749.

The confusion matrix contained 1 true-negative,
7 false-positive, 14 false-negative, and 18
true-positive independent sites. The comparatively high
number of false-positive predictions indicates difficulty
in separating shallot signatures from spectrally similar
non-shallot land-cover and crop conditions.

These findings should therefore be interpreted as a
site-level baseline assessment rather than as evidence for
operational estimation of shallot-planted area.


DISCUSSION NOTES

The site-level evaluation reveals substantial uncertainty
that would have been obscured by a conventional random
pixel-level train-test split. In particular, specificity
was only 0.125, indicating
that the model frequently assigned high shallot probability
to non-shallot reference sites.

This behavior is consistent with spectral overlap among
vegetated agricultural surfaces in a single seasonal
Sentinel-2 composite. Reflectance and vegetation-index
values alone may not uniquely represent crop identity when
different crops occupy similar phenological states.

The results also emphasize the importance of reference-data
quality. A limited number of independent non-shallot sites
reduces the diversity of background conditions represented
during model fitting and increases uncertainty in
specificity estimates. Consequently, the current
classification should not be interpreted as an operational
shallot-area map.

Future improvement should prioritize independently verified
reference polygons, additional non-shallot agricultural
sites, and multi-temporal features describing phenological
trajectories. These improvements are more important than
extensive hyperparameter optimization of the current
training set.
